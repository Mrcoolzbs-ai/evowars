## 2.32 MAVEN upper atmosphere: NGIMS L2 quality-aware ingestion and IUVS O/NO references

**NGIMS — NASA PDS Atmospheres Node.** Archive landing page: https://pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/MAVEN/ngims.html; query interface: https://pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/MAVEN/ngims_data_search.html. NGIMS archive coverage is listed through 2025-02-15. The SIS (MAVEN-NGIMS-SIS-0001, Rev. 1.13, 2022-10-30) defines L2 neutral single-species abundance fields `alt` [km], `species`, `abundance` [part/cm3], `precision` [0..1, 1σ percent error], and `quality`; the L3 product instead contains altitude-resampled density bins and quality flags. The ingestion contract is implemented in `src/modules/Data/mavenUpperAtmosphereEvidence.ts`:
- `parseMavenNgimsL2NeutralCsvRow()` accepts the documented L2 column names;
- `validateMavenNgimsL2NeutralRow()` converts abundance ×10^6 to part/m3 and calculates the absolute one-sigma density uncertainty as density × relative precision;
- `IV/OV` are accepted as verified, `IU/OU` are rejected by default (explicit opt-in only), and `UR` is always rejected;
- `buildMavenNgimsNeutralProfile()` requires one species, one orbit, one TID, one inbound/outbound branch, one quality class and unique altitudes. It sorts samples by altitude but does not average or splice tracks.
A single NGIMS density row/profile is not an initial world state: it lacks co-located full T(z), P(z), bulk composition and other species, and retains the individual orbit geometry.

**NO instrument caveat.** Stevens et al. (2019), DOI 10.1029/2019JE005945, report NGIMS NO around 3×10^6 cm−3 at 155 km but explicitly note a possible high bias from recombination of atomic N and O inside the instrument (based on communication from the NGIMS team). The reader therefore attaches `possible_instrument_chemistry_bias` and a warning to any NGIMS species row named `NO`; a verified IV/OV quality flag does not remove this chemistry-interference caveat. No NGIMS NO profile is imported as a trusted chemical abundance.

**IUVS NO dayglow retrieval — Stevens et al. (2019), DOI 10.1029/2019JE005945.** The paper averages nine sequential IUVS limb profiles from 6–8 April 2016 (MY33, Ls=133–134°, approximately 53°N, local time ≈18 h, SZA ≈75°). NO number density is retrieved from limb radiance by onion-peeling inversion from 128 down to 80 km in 8-km increments; the uppermost positive-radiance layer assumes a 10-km emission scale height above it. The article shows the retrieved profile in Figure 6 but does not give a numerical table of density values in its text. The ±30% systematic is the reported MUV radiance uncertainty; natural 1σ variability of the nine-profile mean is propagated via Monte Carlo. These are not a full node covariance matrix. The code stores the observation geometry, altitude grid, retrieval method and uncertainty semantics, but deliberately sets `numericalDensityTableIncluded=false`; no graph-read values are passed off as tabulated data. The article used public IUVS L1b periapse v13 data. The PDS IUVS archive page currently states that derived density profiles are not yet archived as a derived product.

**Atomic O — Ritter et al. (2019), DOI 10.1029/2019JA026669.** At 130 km and Ls≈350°, the publication derives model-constrained [O]/[CO2] ratios of 3.1% (MY32) and 3.0% (MY33) after applying different scaling factors to model CO2/O densities to match IUVS limb observations. The unscaled model ratios are 2.9% and 2.5%, respectively. These are not direct NGIMS in-situ densities and not a vertical point profile. The paper notes factor-of-two uncertainty in derived atomic-O density due to surface-recombination processes, CO2 density uncertainty up to about a factor 1.5, and IUVS FUV absolute calibration reliability around ±25%; it does not publish a numerical 1σ for the final O/CO2 ratios. The separate reference record preserves model/scaling values and does not promote them to exact initial mixing ratios.

**Non-merging rule:** the IUVS NO mean (MY33 / April 2016), O/CO2 model-constrained ratios (MY32/MY33 / Ls≈350°), NGIMS single-orbit profiles, MGS 1998 occultation, SAM near-surface annual means, ACS HO2 upper limits and OpenMARS model ranges remain separate evidence objects. The code has not built a single contemporaneous O/NO/HO2 profile and does not inject these observations into Scheduler chemistry.


**Whole-file parsing:** `parseMavenNgimsL2NeutralCsvText()` processes complete ASCII CSV text, including BOM, CRLF/LF, quoted fields and escaped quotes. It requires the documented L2 column set and unique header names. The file-level result distinguishes malformed CSV/header from per-record validation: every data row keeps its record number and a verified/uncertain/rejected result, so unreliable or malformed rows remain auditable rather than being silently removed. This reader is covered with controlled fixture tests; no fixture is represented as an actual MAVEN orbit observation.


**Archive index identity:** The PDS logs directory lists `MAVEN_NGIMS_l2_del1-35.csv` as the L2 neutral index (revision del1-35, last modified 2023-11-15; 5.2 MB in the directory listing): https://pdsatmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/MAVEN/logs/MAVEN_NGIMS_l2_del1-35.csv. This identifier is recorded in `MAVEN_NGIMS_PDS_ARCHIVE.archiveIndexFiles` as a retrieval locator. The index itself and its per-orbit row payloads have not yet been frozen in the repository; the CSV parser's test fixtures are explicitly synthetic.


**Single-profile selection after whole-file import:** `buildMavenNgimsNeutralProfileFromCsvImport()` requires explicit selection of species, MAVEN orbit, NGIMS TID, NGIMS focus mode, inbound/outbound branch, and verified/uncertain quality class. Only accepted measurements matching all six selectors reach the monotonic profile builder; cross-species/orbit/TID/focus-mode/direction/quality mixtures and duplicate altitudes remain rejected. This is an import/profile extraction helper only: no generated world, reservoir, or Scheduler state is mutated.

## 2.34 Mars photolysis reference values: Taysum & Palmer (2020), Table B2

**Source:** Taysum, B. M. & Palmer, P. I. (2020), *Photochemistry of Methane and Ethane in the Martian Atmosphere*, Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets, DOI https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006491. The open article describes a one-dimensional LMD-UK-derived photochemistry model. Its inorganic scheme uses a Mars-adapted TUV radiation model and precomputed look-up values. The nine photolysis channels visibly tabulated in Appendix Table B2 are imported into `src/modules/Data/marsPhotochemistryEvidence.ts` with both published altitude nodes (0.50 km and 42.67 km), retaining exact zero entries.

Table B2 lists these first-order photolysis frequencies J [s⁻¹]:

| Channel | J at 0.50 km | J at 42.67 km |
|---|---:|---:|
| O2 + hν → O + O | 2.95×10⁻¹⁰ | 5.16×10⁻⁹ |
| O2 + hν → O + O(1D) | 0 | 0 |
| CO2 + hν → CO + O | 3.79×10⁻¹² | 1.02×10⁻¹⁰ |
| CO2 + hν → CO + O(1D) | 0 | 0 |
| O3 + hν → O2 + O(1D) | 3.12×10⁻³ | 4.09×10⁻³ |
| O3 + hν → O2 + O | 5.22×10⁻⁴ | 6.86×10⁻⁴ |
| H2O + hν → H + OH | 1.28×10⁻¹⁰ | 4.67×10⁻⁸ |
| H2O2 + hν → OH + OH | 3.93×10⁻⁵ | 5.26×10⁻⁵ |
| HO2 + hν → O + OH | 2.40×10⁻⁴ | 3.20×10⁻⁴ |

The table note specifies SZA=0° and solar longitude Ls=251° (perihelion). In the paper's method section, the TUV look-up values are described as depending additionally on atmospheric temperature, O3 column, total atmospheric column, Sun–Mars distance and optical opacity/dust loading. The abbreviated table does not provide a machine-readable spectral radiation field or enough information to reconstruct these dependencies at arbitrary altitude/time. These values are **model look-up outputs**, not observations, not universal photolysis constants and not a complete photochemical network. The article's method section says there are ten inorganic photolysis reactions, whereas the accessible Table B2 shows nine rows; the data module preserves the nine table rows and explicitly does not invent a tenth channel.

`evaluateMarsPhotolysisAtReferenceNode()` computes only the local volumetric event rate `J × n(parent)` at one of the exact two published altitude nodes, where the caller provides a non-negative reactant number density [particles cm⁻³] with identified provenance. It returns the stoichiometric net rates for the tabulated channel in [particles cm⁻³ s⁻¹], preserves separate provenance for J and n, and flags uncertainty as `not_quantified` because the paper does not report a 1σ for these tabulated J values. It rejects unknown channels, non-finite/negative density, missing density provenance and all off-grid altitudes. No altitude interpolation, extrapolation, inferred density, profile reconstruction or Scheduler mutation occurs; output explicitly carries `schedulerEligible=false`.

This is a quantitative building block for later source/sink coupling, not closure of atmospheric photochemistry: the Taysum radiation scenario is not time/space-co-registered to the MGS 1998 occultation, SAM MY31–MY34 surface means, ACS HO2 MY36–MY37 upper limits or any not-yet-imported MAVEN orbit. These unlike data sets must not be combined into one initial atmosphere without a synchronized, sourced forcing/profile. Tests verify the exact table values, elemental balance of all nine tabulated channels, `J×n` dimensional arithmetic, exact-node-only behavior and fail-closed provenance/validation.


## 2.35 Mars gas-phase reaction rates: Taysum & Palmer (2020), Appendix Table B1

**Source:** Taysum, B. M. & Palmer, P. I. (2020), *Photochemistry of Methane and Ethane in the Martian Atmosphere*, JGR: Planets, DOI https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006491. The 32 gas-phase reaction rows in Appendix Table B1 are recorded in `src/modules/Data/marsGasPhaseKinetics.ts`. The catalogue stores typed reactant/product stoichiometry, the published rate expression, the reaction-specific cited rate reference, and the article DOI. It does not parse the displayed reaction string to infer stoichiometry.

### Supported rate forms and explicit limits

- **29 rows** carry direct evaluable expressions: 28 Arrhenius expressions in two-body or three-body form, plus the one 298 K-only row described below. The evaluator applies `k(T)=A(T/T_ref)^n exp(B/T)`; the sign of B is retained exactly as published in the table.
- **Two fall-off rows, c11 and c17,** preserve the exact `k_3rd(...)` strings but are not evaluated. The paper's reproduction of the formula places a 2.5 CO2 efficiency factor in the k0 argument, while the separate reproduced Mars-model equation (Taysum thesis Eq. 3.6) also multiplies k0[M] by 2.5. The current import deliberately treats this as an unresolved convention; it neither double-counts nor silently removes that factor.
- **Row e1** (OH + CO → CO2 + H) supplies only a single rate value of 1.47×10⁻¹³ cm³ molecule⁻¹ s⁻¹ at 298 K and points to Joshi & Wang (2006) for further details. The evaluator therefore permits this number only at exactly 298 K, not as a general Arrhenius law.

The article states that CO2 is the third body on Mars (rather than the terrestrial N2 bath gas). For the direct termolecular rows, the collision-efficiency multiplier appearing in Table B1 is already embedded in A: 2.075 for a1 and 2.5 for the other direct termolecular rows. The caller still has to supply the local CO2 number density explicitly in particles cm⁻³. The data/evaluator never infer [CO2] from pressure, mixing ratios, total number density or the SAM subset. Direct termolecular rate constants have units cm⁶ molecule⁻² s⁻¹ and their volumetric event rate is k[A][B][CO2], particles cm⁻³ s⁻¹; bimolecular rows use cm³ molecule⁻¹ s⁻¹ and k[A][B].

The table does not publish per-reaction formal temperature-validity intervals or numerical 1σ uncertainties. Consequently, non-e1 evaluated rows return `temperatureValidity='source_interval_not_published'` and `uncertaintyStatus='not_quantified'`; their formulas are not represented as having a validated universal range. The module validates finite positive T and explicit non-negative reactant/third-body densities, checks the elemental balance of all 32 rows, and rejects unsupported c11/c17 and e1 at any T other than 298 K. It does not activate an ODE network or Scheduler state.

Sources: the article's Appendix Table B1 https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006491; Taysum's thesis, Chapter 3.2, especially the CO2 third-body efficiency discussion and Eq. 3.6 (University of Edinburgh repository: https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/abbe0e3a-e902-4984-b853-e6e6d8e3fba6/content). Table B1 lists 32 rows; the two unsupported fall-off forms and single-temperature e1 are preserved as explicit non-executable data rather than completed with assumptions.


## 2.36 Coupled Mars gas-phase + photolysis concentration integrator (24 Nov 2026)

`src/modules/ChemicalEvolution/marsPhotochemicalNetwork.ts` is a local single-compartment ODE adapter over the evidence tables in §§2.34–2.35. It accepts an explicit complete vector of all 16 gas/radical species in particles cm⁻³, a separately sourced constant temperature, one exact photolysis table altitude node (0.50 or 42.67 km), explicitly selected reaction IDs, and a physical duration in seconds. The initial-state and temperature provenance are independently required and preserved. No atmospheric column inventory is silently converted to volume concentration.

At build time every selected rate is evaluated from its source table at the explicit input. Photolysis rates are fixed to the source table scenario SZA=0° and Ls=251° at the selected node. Gas-phase termolecular reactions use the **current dynamic CO2 state** as [M]; the collision-efficiency coefficient is already embedded in the direct Table B1 pre-exponential as documented above. Thus the single compartment integrates mass-action gas reactions and first-order photolysis together without importing the separate N2-bath-gas KIDA mechanism.

The adapter builds a 16-component stiff ODE and solves it using the repository's existing 3-stage fifth-order L-stable Radau IIA implementation. It supplies an analytical Jacobian, non-negative state constraints, and explicit linear invariants for C, H and O. Selected reaction stoichiometries are independently checked before solving. Any missing concentration, invalid provenance, unknown or unsupported reaction (including c11/c17; and e1 unless T=298 K), unsupported photolysis altitude, or solver failure rejects the whole step; no partial concentration state is returned. Test concentration fixtures are expressly synthetic and are not presented as observations.

This closes a missing *local reaction + photolysis integration primitive*, not the Stage 4.2 physical atmosphere. The adapter is deliberately not connected to GeneratedWorld, Scheduler, ChemicalEvolutionState, or the existing vertical eddy-diffusion operator. It has no vertical profile, no transport, no time-dependent T/SZA/Ls/dust/opacity, no surface fluxes, no condensation, no uncertainty propagation, and no synchronized observation-based radical initial inventory. It therefore cannot be used to claim a self-consistent simulated Martian atmosphere. Those remain separate blockers to Stage 4.2 closure.

## 2.35 Martian inorganic gas-phase kinetics and single-compartment photochemical network

**Source:** Taysum & Palmer (2020), *Photochemistry of Methane and Ethane in the Martian Atmosphere*, JGR: Planets 125, e2020JE006491, DOI https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006491. The article describes an LMD-UK-derived 1-D Mars photochemistry module, with a 25-layer atmosphere extending from the surface to approximately 70 km, time-dependent solar geometry, vertical tracer diffusion and an implicit chemistry solver. Table B1 lists 32 gas-phase inorganic reaction rows; Table B2 lists nine explicit photolysis rows (while the method text refers to ten photolysis reactions).

The canonical gas-phase source catalogue is `src/modules/Data/marsGasPhaseKinetics.ts`. It preserves all 32 Table B1 rows:
- 29 rows have explicit Arrhenius expressions (24 bimolecular channels and five termolecular Arrhenius channels);
- c11 and c17 are retained as `falloff_not_evaluated` because the printed `k_3rd` expressions do not unambiguously resolve whether the CO2 efficiency factor 2.5 is already folded into the low-pressure parameter; no double-counting or guessed interpretation is made;
- e1 (OH + CO → CO2 + H) retains the single published rate value at exactly 298 K and is rejected at every other temperature.

For termolecular chemistry, Mars CO2 is the bath gas. The article states that the model applies a CO2 collision-efficiency correction relative to N2/dry air; the catalogue retains the table's printed pre-exponential coefficients (including the distinct 2.075 factor in a1) and requires an explicit local [CO2] in particles cm⁻³ at evaluation time. It does not infer [CO2] from pressure, mixing ratio or a column inventory. The current `falloff_not_evaluated` status is specific to c11/c17; the other five termolecular rows are evaluated with their explicit CO2 third-body contract.

The rate evaluator `evaluateMarsGasPhaseReaction()` calculates the printed rate law and event rate for one selected record, returning stoichiometric net species source/sink rates. Tests preserve all 32 records, verify C/H/O balance for every row, cover explicit [CO2] for termolecular channels, retain catalytic species as zero-net rows, check e1's single-point-only behavior, and reject unresolved fall-off parameters. Table B1 does not provide individual 1σ uncertainties or per-record numeric temperature intervals; the catalogue reports uncertainty as not quantified and does not assert arbitrary-temperature scientific validity.

The photolysis catalogue is `src/modules/Data/marsPhotochemistryEvidence.ts` and stores all nine rows visible in Table B2 at exactly 0.50 km and 42.67 km. The model reference is SZA=0° and Ls=251° (perihelion). These are precomputed modified-TUV lookup values that also depend on atmospheric temperature, CO2/O3 columns, Sun–Mars distance and dust/optical opacity, not universal constants. No tenth photolysis row is invented where the method text and displayed Table B2 row count differ. The source does not give a numerical 1σ for those J values.

`src/modules/ChemicalEvolution/marsPhotochemicalNetwork.ts` is the canonical integration path. It combines an explicitly selected subset of gas-phase Table B1 reactions with an explicitly selected subset of Table B2 photolysis reactions in one 16-species concentration vector, with analytical Jacobian, C/H/O linear invariants, non-negativity checks and the existing Radau IIA solver. It supports the 29 Arrhenius rows and e1 at exactly 298 K; c11/c17 remain blocked. Photolysis uses one exact Table B2 altitude node, a fixed SZA/Ls lookup scenario and a constant temperature for the requested interval. The caller must provide all 16 initial number densities and identified state/temperature provenance. The network uses a single homogeneous compartment; no conversion from column inventories is attempted.

Regression tests now exercise all 24 bimolecular k(T) expressions against their independent published 298 K values (within 1% for source rounding), and integrate the complete executable subset of 30 gas-phase records (29 Arrhenius plus e1 at 298 K) with all nine B2 photolysis channels in the synthetic 16-species fixture. They verify non-negative output and C/H/O conservation. The test fixture is explicitly synthetic and is not a measured atmosphere.

**Hard limits:** this is a source-backed single-compartment numerical model component, not a complete observation-constrained 1-D Mars atmosphere. It does not yet import or construct a contemporaneous observed full vertical profile, does not turn NGIMS/MGS/SAM/ACS observations from different years/locations into one synchronized state, does not evolve solar zenith angle or dust opacity during the step, and does not include vertical transport, surface boundary fluxes, condensation, thermal feedback or a scheduler/world commit. Initial radical densities must be caller-supplied with provenance; this model does not invent them. These restrictions remain Stage 4.2 blockers.

## Exact cal-mkr phase-start MKID to cal-hk telemetry association — TID 14282 / orbit 391 (25.11.2026)

**Association key.** The cal-hk detached PDS4 label defines `MKID` as
"the numeric ID of the most recent marker to occur at TIME". The HK parser
therefore assigns a row to a phase only if its `MKID` is exactly equal to
the marker ID of that phase's `Phase mass scans starting` record in
cal-mkr. Rows with the phase's `Phase ended` marker ID are counted
separately as post-phase context, not assigned to the phase. Blank IDs and
all other marker IDs remain unassigned.

**Clock policy.** HK `TIME` has units SECONDS and is described by PDS as a
spacecraft-clock counter approximated by the NGIMS internal clock. It is
retained as raw source context but is not compared to cal-mkr TIME or L1B

does not convert SCLK to UTC, estimate a clock offset, use a time window,
perform nearest-neighbour matching, interpolate, or carry a previous
telemetry value forward. The cal-hk label also says empty field values
mean that the field was not read and that no interpolation is performed.

units from the exact PDS4 Field_Delimited entries: open/closed-source

and filament selector. Units are not inferred from channel names.

## MAVEN NGIMS calibration evidence and applicability (Stage 4.2)

### Closed-source ground reference is not flight validation

The current controlled PDS SIS is Rev. 1.13 (2022-10-30). Table 3 gives
pre-flight ground sensitivities for 40Ar and 84Kr at 50 and 250 microampere
emission settings for both filament/CEM pairs. The table footnote explicitly
says the sensitivities were to be updated following the first in-flight
calibration. The eight values are preserved in
`src/modules/Data/mavenNgimsL1bCalibrationReference.ts` and the machine-readable
reference copy `mavenNgimsL1bCalibrationReference.json`; they are labelled
`preflight_ground_reference`, not flight-calibrated coefficients.

The closed-source equation is
`S_a = S_n * C_RF(m_s) * sigma_s`, with `C_RF = 0.71` for mass-to-charge
at or below 20.5 and 1.0 above 20.5. The module provides this equation only as
a labelled ground-reference calculation. It has no CPS-to-density operation.

### Dead time and detector identity

SIS Rev. 1.13 Table 2 provides dead-time coefficients for CEM 1, but not CEM 2.
Stone et al. (2018) also publish a study-specific model. Neither model is
automatically applied to TID 14282. L1B `CPS_DT` is already dead-time corrected,
and `CPS_DT_BKGD` is both background-subtracted and dead-time corrected; SIS
recommends the latter for science computations. Applying another dead-time
correction to those L1B fields would double-correct the data.

### Flight evidence that is useful but narrow

Stone et al. (2018) report an in-flight known-composition calibration-gas
fragmentation ratio for nitrogen, m/z 14 relative to m/z 28, of
3.47 ± 0.42% from six calibration-gas measurements. It is retained as a
nitrogen-specific response result, not a general fragmentation matrix.
The same study applies a detector gain-stabilization density factor of 1.5331
to orbits after 748; TID 14282 is orbit 391, so this correction is not applied.

The PDS SIS states that the internal calibration reservoir is opened near
apoapsis at intervals of several weeks, introducing a restricted capillary
flow into the open source. The open-source sensitivity equation still requires
paired dead-time-corrected reference-gas count rates from both sources and
the energy correction factor `C_EN`. No numerical `C_EN` was found in the
retrieved SIS or cited public analysis sources, so it remains null.

### TID 14282: early-mission applicability guard

A 2017 NGIMS analysis reports that October-December 2014 used different
operating settings than the February 2015 onward period. It describes an
early-December contamination issue at m/z 19 and 16 and subsequent setting
changes, including a reported multiplier setting change from 125 V to 25 V.
TID 14282 is 2014-12-11, orbit 391, and therefore requires manual
configuration review before applying any generic post-February-2015
calibration.

The event HK product records actual scientific readbacks
`QMS:MT_MU1_VCTL = -45.0 V` and `QMS:MT_MU2_VCTL = -2437.6 V` in 23
sparse observations of each field. SIS identifies these as the negative
electrode voltages for Multiplier 1 and Multiplier 2. The event message log
records Multiplier 2 being turned on with command value 2462. These
observations establish the reported electrode settings and active channel;
the retrieved sources do not map the paper's 125/25 V setting to these exact
PDS channels. The two voltage descriptions are therefore not equated.
